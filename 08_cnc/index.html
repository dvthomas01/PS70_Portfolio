<div class="textcontainer"> <p class="margin"></p> <h2 class="main-header">Week 8: CNC Machining</h2> <p class="margin"></p> <h4 class="section-header">Assignment: CNC and Post Processing</h4> <div class="section-container"> <p>This week’s brief was to <b>design something and make it on CNC</b>, then <b>post process</b> it through a follow on process such as molding and casting, vacuum forming, or composites. I completed the <b>ShopBot</b> cut on <b>1/2 inch MDF</b>, sanded the plaque into a clean headband plate, and finished the week with <b>vacuum forming</b> so the routed piece picked up a thin plastic skin.</p> </div> <h4 class="section-header">Concept: Hidden Leaf Headband</h4> <div class="section-container"> <p>I wanted this week’s part to feel personal, not random stock geometry. Anime was a big part of how I grew up, and <b>Naruto</b> was one of the first shows that really stuck with me. In that world, ninjas wear <b>ninja headbands</b> stamped with their village symbol. Naruto is from the <b>Hidden Leaf</b>, so his metal plate shows the leaf emblem. I decided to CNC that emblem as a headband style plaque: a tribute to something that mattered to me, not just a test shape.</p> <div class="center-column"> <img class="project-img" src="./headband_reference.png" alt="Reference image for the Hidden Leaf headband symbol"> </div> <p class="caption">Reference I started from (Hidden Leaf symbol, headband plate).</p> </div> <h4 class="section-header">From Reference to Toolpath: Illustrator Trace, Not Hand Splines</h4> <div class="section-container"> <p>I first tried to redraw the plate in <b>Fusion</b> from scratch. The real prop is <b>curved</b> and full of <b>organic</b> bumps and fillets that are awkward to capture as clean parametric CAD. Chasing every tangent by hand with splines would have eaten the whole week. Instead I took my reference into <b>Adobe Illustrator</b>, used <b>Image Trace</b> to vectorize the artwork, and exported an <b>SVG</b>. From there I converted to <b>DXF</b> for the ShopBot workflow.</p> <div class="center-column"> <img class="project-img" src="./svg_traceout_headband.png" alt="Vector trace of the headband artwork from Illustrator"> </div> <p class="caption">Traced vector artwork (screenshot of the SVG trace result) before DXF export.</p> </div> <h4 class="section-header">ShopBot: Setup, Cut versus Engrave, and MDF Run</h4> <div class="section-container"> <p>Uploading the DXF was only the start. Learning which ShopBot operations meant <b>through cut</b> versus <b>engrave</b>, how to set a shallow depth for the “etched” areas so it read like the show, and how the control software wanted geometry grouped took <b>time and trial and error</b>. Once I was happy with the toolpath preview, I fixtured <b>1/2 inch MDF</b> and ran the job. It ran long enough that I had to be patient, but when the spindle finished, the plate was clearly readable as the Leaf symbol.</p> <div class="center-column"> <img class="project-img" src="./web/shopbot_visualize_cutout.jpg" alt="ShopBot toolpath or preview of the headband cut"> </div> <p class="caption">ShopBot preview, setup snapshot from my cut.</p> </div> <h4 class="section-header">Result, Defects, and Finish</h4> <div class="section-container"> <p>Overall I was <b>happy with the cut</b>. The main imperfections I noticed were in the <b>small circular engravings at the four corners</b>: the design expects little <b>raised points</b> inside those rings. On my board, the <b>two features on the right side</b> lost those tips. The cutter clipped them. I am treating that as a lesson in how fine engraving detail translates to a given tool diameter and depth, not a reason to scrap the piece.</p> <p>After the machine stopped, I <b>sanded the outer profile</b> to soften the corners and clean up fuzz from the MDF fibers. That was enough for a display piece I am proud to keep on the shelf.</p> <p>Once I had that <b>MDF headband plate</b> where I wanted it, I moved straight into <b>post processing</b>. The next section covers how I used the lab vacuum former to pull plastic over the same geometry.</p> <div class="center-column"> <img class="project-img" src="./web/headband_final_product.jpg" alt="Finished CNC headband plaque on MDF after sanding"> </div> <p class="caption">Finished MDF headband plaque after sanding.</p> </div> <h4 class="section-header">Post Processing: Vacuum Forming</h4> <div class="section-container"> <p>For my post processing I decided on <b>vacuum forming</b> so I could give the headband some <b>protective casing</b> and a more <b>lustrous</b> look than bare MDF. I picked up a <b>sheet of plastic</b>, went to the <b>vacuum former</b>, and got a <b>tutorial</b> on how to run the machine safely and get a clean pull.</p> <p>Once the plastic was heated to an <b>adequate temperature</b>, I <b>pressed it down</b> against my headband and <b>vacuum sealed</b> it so the sheet would collapse onto the routed shape and pick up its detail. When that step was finished, the plastic had <b>formed around the headband</b>.</p> <div class="center-column"> <img class="project-img" src="./web/vacuum_former.jpg" alt="Vacuum former with heated plastic sheet forming over the MDF headband"> </div> <p class="caption">Heating and forming the sheet over the MDF headband on the vacuum former.</p> <p>I then <b>trimmed off the excess plastic</b> so there was no messy flash hanging past the emblem and the skin would <b>fit neatly and tightly</b> around the headband plate instead of looking like a loose bubble.</p> <div class="image-grid-2"> <img class="project-img" src="./web/headband_out_of_the_vacuum_former.jpg" alt="Vacuum formed plastic shell on the headband after forming"> <img class="project-img" src="./web/trimmed_and_final_headband.jpg" alt="Trimmed vacuum formed headband with excess plastic removed"> </div> <p class="caption">Right after forming, then after trimming so the plastic sits tight on the piece.</p> <p>After I trimmed away the extra material, I considered the <b>vacuum forming step complete</b>. My <b>final product</b> was the formed headband with the plastic sealed cleanly around it.</p> </div> </div>
